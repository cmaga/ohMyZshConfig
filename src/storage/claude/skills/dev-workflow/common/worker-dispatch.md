# Dispatch workers

Workers arrive to a scaffolded repo with failing tests. Their job is to fill bodies until the tests pass — not to decide anything.

- Spawn one `worker-agent` per task, passing the card's model id verbatim as the `model` opt — `haiku`, `sonnet`, or `opus`, never an archetype or alias name, and never `fable` ([archetypes](../references/archetypes.md)). Record each worker's `agentId` from its spawn result.
- Dispatch in waves from each card's **After** field: wave 1 is every card with `After: none`; dispatch a wave's cards in parallel, wait for it to finish, then dispatch the cards whose `After` blockers are now complete.
- Two cards in one wave touching the same file is a plan defect, not something to patch here. Stop, fix the ordering in the plan, then dispatch.
- **One writer per worktree at a time — per tree, not per file.** Disjoint file assignments are not isolation: a working tree is a single mutable object, and `git reset`, `checkout --`, `stash`, `clean` and `restore` act on all of it whatever the assignment said. Two workers with no file overlap at all have lost forty-five verified edits to one `reset`. Fan out freely for *reading* — classification, measurement, review, verification — and keep every write in one worker or in your own hands.
- **Say in the card that workers may not run those commands.** A worker told it owns seven files reasonably concludes it may run git; it cannot. One that needs to undo its own work reverts it by editing.
- **Name the siblings in every card when concurrent workers are live.** The evidence for a hostile foreign process and for a sibling worker is identical — reflog entries it did not issue, files it did not write — and the correct response to an intruder is to stop. One worker reasoned correctly to the wrong conclusion and halted, which cost an hour of work the manager could have prevented with one sentence.
- **Tell each worker to commit in coherent pieces as it goes.** Sessions die and only committed work survives one; staging alone is cheap insurance in a shared tree, and staged changes are the only ones that survived the `reset` above.
- **Every card says the worker spawns nothing** — in the second person: *anything you spawn reports to me, not to you; do the work yourself.* A worker is the last level, and a helper it dispatches reports past both of you to the main session, leaving the worker blocked on an answer that was delivered somewhere else. A card too large for one worker gets split, never subcontracted.
- Each worker's prompt is the T-N task card from the plan — paste that block, not the whole plan. The worker can Read `.claude-artifacts/workflows/dev-workflow/plan.md` if it needs to disambiguate.
- **Resume an escalating worker in preference to replacing it.** When a worker stops and escalates, send your decision to its `agentId` with `SendMessage`. It keeps every file it read and every decision it made; a fresh worker re-reads all of it and re-derives state you already paid for.
  - **If the resume itself fails, replace the worker — do not halt.** A refused, errored, or unreachable `SendMessage` is a broken channel, not a broken task. Dispatch a fresh `worker-agent` with the original card plus your decision plus what the escalating worker reported, and say in the exit report that the task was restarted rather than resumed. Resume is an optimisation; finishing the work is the requirement. Two failed resumes is the cap — replace on the third attempt rather than spending more rounds on the channel.
  - **Replace rather than resume when the worker's context is spent**, even though the channel is fine. Resuming hands the work that is left an exhausted budget, and its committed work is what a replacement actually needs — the branch is the handoff artifact, not the transcript.
  - A worker cannot message you back. It reaches you only by ending its turn, so its returned report is the whole handoff: it must carry the question, what it already did, and what a replacement would need. Write that expectation into the card.
- A worker that reports a test it believes is wrong stops there. Read the test and the scaffold yourself, decide, then resume that worker with your decision — never tell it to work around the test.
