# Lever impact

One number per lever, 0-100: how much degrading this lever degrades the final deliverable. Session model = 100 is the anchor — never step it lightly. A type has two levers, its model and its effort, rated separately; (est.) marks a number no benchmark separates.

| Lever | Impact | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Session model | 100 | Defines the entire solution space — plan, scope, tests, review standards. Every downstream token executes its decisions, so its errors compound through the whole ticket. Nothing gates it. Independent Terminal-Bench 4.0 (Artificial Analysis at high / Vals.ai, 2026-11-18): Opus 5.5 56.6 / 65.2, Fable 5.2 59.6 / 65.2, Sonnet 5.5 43.9 / 64.1. Fable 5.2 at high scores what Opus 5.5 scores at xhigh (59.6) at 2.62x against 1.75x, and ties it on Vals.ai, so fable→opus is a free step on coding evidence. The 100 covers the full span down to haiku. |
| Review model | 60 | The adversary, verify, and taste roles: the interrogate station's adversaries and the verify passes before a PR, whose misses ship, the verification lanes and the verdict, a miss in which nothing downstream catches, and the tasters. Strong review is also what makes cheap workers safe — degrading it weakens every other lever's safety argument. |
| Judgment model | 60 | The judgment and escalation roles: designs, syntheses, judges, and the calls that would otherwise reach the user, which land unmediated, so a weak call becomes design direction nobody reviews. |
| Session effort | 50 | Same blast radius as session model, but shallower per step: published data shows max and xhigh equivalent — on Opus 5.5, independent Terminal-Bench 4.0 is 59.6 at both xhigh and max, 56.6 at high, 52.5 at medium and 31.3 at low (Artificial Analysis, 2026-11-18) — while max costs 2.90x high per task, xhigh 1.75x and medium, Opus 5.5's default, 0.71x (Index v5.0.1). On Opus, steps down to medium move quality less than model swaps; low is a cliff. Fable 5.2 is the flattest: 54.5 at low against 61.6 at xhigh, at a fifth of the cost. |
| Critical code model | 45 (est.) | The hardest and contract roles, and code on an Extra effort path: cross-cutting design, concurrency, subtle algorithms, where a weak model's mistakes are hardest to see, and the tests written before the code, which confine the coder. The bug review and the verdict gate the code, but a weak contract lets weak code through them. |
| General model | 40 | Every role no other type covers, the largest share of agents (one measured run: 31 of 62 agents, 46% of output tokens). |
| Review effort | 35 | Thoroughness is the whole job of an adversary and an auditor; lower effort trims exactly the double-checking the roles exist for. Driving a done-when line is effort-insensitive; auditing a diff against its intent is not. |
| Code model | 30 | Runs every code delegate. A brief is fully specified inside a solved problem and every diff passes the quality gate and the verdict, but a model that does exactly what it was told misses what a brief leaves unstated, and no test catches that. `deepseek-flash` serves DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash-1017; `deepseek-v4-pro` serves DeepSeek-V4-Pro-0813. Independent Terminal-Bench 4.0 (Artificial Analysis / Vals.ai, 2026-11-18): flash at max 33.8 / 31.8, pro — / 14.1, against Opus 5.5 59.6 at xhigh / 65.2 and Sonnet 5.5 57.1 at xhigh, 43.9 at high / 64.1. Independent coding runs put flash 26-33 points behind Opus 5.5, so vendor-run scores never rank this lever. Pro trails flash at 3x its cost per task, so flash dominates pro. Sonnet 5.5 beats flash by 23 points at xhigh (1.18x) and by 10 at high (0.47x). Opus 5.5 at high (1.00x, 56.6) costs less than Sonnet 5.5 at xhigh (1.18x, 57.1) for half a point less. Haiku 5.5 is dominated: at max it scores 32.8 / 36.4, level with flash, and past a 100K-token prompt costs 0.59x, more than Sonnet 5.5 at high. |
| Code effort | 30 | Sonnet 5.5, the code type's model, is the most effort-sensitive option measured: Terminal-Bench 4.0 falls from 63.6 at max to 57.1 at xhigh and 43.9 at high (Artificial Analysis, 2026-11-18). One step from xhigh to high costs more than the Opus→Sonnet swap at xhigh (2.5 points). Contract tests bound what a worker ships, not how much of the brief it gets right. `inherit` gives the model's own effort, not the session's (Claude Code 2.1.317). |
| Judgment effort | 30 (est.) | The judgment and escalation roles, shallower per step: the model-to-effort ratio the other paired levers show. |
| Critical code effort | 20 (est.) | The critical code roles, shallower per step, for the same reason. |
| General effort | 15 (est.) | Every role no other type covers, shallower per step. |
| Other family model | 15 (est.) | The second opinion from another model family: the final review before every PR, one of the interrogate station's adversaries, the arena's cross-judge, and the decision trail's reviewer. Its value is that it fails differently from the author; a session-family model beside it carries the review. |
| Research model | 8 | The explore role: read-only search and source reading, work every tier is saturated on. Output is intermediate and a judgment-role agent or the session synthesizes it, so a cheap model here is near-free, the safest lever to cheapen first. |
| Research effort | 3 (est.) | Read-only search, shallower per step than its model. |
| Advisor model | 35 (est.) | Consulted at decision points — before committing to an approach, on a recurring error, before declaring done — so it moves plan quality, the thing the session model decides. It reaches only sessions and agents whose model ranks at or below it, and Claude decides when to call it, so its reach per run is unmeasured. Unbenchmarked estimate. Off by position, not disabled. |
