# Look It Up

Apply when you choose a stack, library, architecture, or pattern, or rely on how an outside tool, API, or platform behaves. What you know stops at your training cutoff and does not feel dated, so a confident default is where stale knowledge does the most harm.

- **Search before you choose, not after it breaks.** Read the current docs and changelog of what you will build on, how similar projects solved the same problem, and recent comparisons for this problem's needs.
- **Weigh what you find against your default.** Keep the default only when the search shows it still fits, and say what the search changed or confirmed.
- **Cite it.** Put the sources in the decision you record, so a reviewer checks the premise instead of trusting your memory.
- **The repo's precedent wins.** What the project already uses stays unless the order is to change it: look up how to use it well, not what to replace it with.

You skipped this when a decision names a library, version, API, or practice with no source behind it, or when its only evidence is what you remembered.

For a design with no precedent in the repo, the [architect](../../commands/cook/stations/architect.md) station's Phase A runs this as research before any sketch.
